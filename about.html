01

WHO AM I?

I like figuring out how things work.

I'm a cybersecurity professional who genuinely enjoys figuring out how things work, breaking concepts down, and getting hands-on with the technical side of security.

I have both my bachelor's and master's degrees, but one thing cybersecurity has definitely taught me is that learning doesn't stop when school does. There's always another tool to understand, another detection to build, another query to troubleshoot, or another rabbit hole to go down.

And honestly, that's a big part of why cyber.fal exists.

02

WHY CYBER.FAL?

This isn't supposed to be a highlight reel.

I wanted somewhere to document what I'm actually learning as I continue growing in cybersecurity.

That includes the labs that work, the ones that don't, the queries I have to relearn, the commands I know I'll forget later, and those moments when a concept finally starts to click.

“

Learning the tool matters. Understanding why you're using it matters more.

You'll see a lot of SOC and blue-team focused content here because that's an area I really enjoy. I'm especially interested in Splunk and SIEM, threat detection, Windows security, threat intelligence, cloud security, automation, and the investigative side of security.

03

THE ANALYST MINDSET

Finding the event is only the beginning.

Finding an event is one thing.

Understanding why it happened, whether it matters, and what I should investigate next is the part I really want to keep getting better at.

That's also why I try not to just post a query and move on.

I want to understand what each part is doing, what the data is actually telling me, how I could turn it into a useful detection or dashboard, and how an analyst could use that information during an investigation.
